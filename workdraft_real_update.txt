1. Introduction: 
Addressing the Evolving Phishing Threat
The fast changes in how businesses use digital tools, like email, online websites, cloud services, and the internet, have led to a big increase in cybercrime. Phishing is still one of the biggest and most expensive types of online attacks around the world. The problem with today's defenses is that they mostly only look at emails, but phishing attacks have changed a lot and are no longer just about sending junk emails. The latest threats use advanced techniques like:
Fraudulent login pages
Fake Websites
QR code scams
Business Email Compromise (BEC)
Malicious attachments
Impersonation techniques
It is also worth noting that the threat landscape in 2025 has expanded to include AI-generated phishing documents, fake invoices crafted by LLMs, and deepfake-assisted voice phishing (vishing). While AEGIS-ONE's current scope addresses digital channel threats (email, browser, QR codes, and web interactions), these emerging vectors are acknowledged and documented as planned future extensions of the platform.
These techniques are made to deceive users into sharing private login details or sending money. Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) are especially at risk because they usually don't have strong enough cybersecurity systems in place.
Most current systems that protect businesses from phishing attacks mainly work by checking emails. While effective at reducing spam and identifying known threats, these solutions often fail to:
Block threats originating outside the email system.
Monitor user behavior in real-time.
Having proper analytics and reporting system
Prevent attacks before credentials are submitted.
Ensure consistent security across various digital platforms.
So, most phishing attacks work because they succeed when users take action, like clicking a link, scanning a harmful QR code, entering login details, or visiting a fake website.

Our Solution: The In-house AI-Based Phishing Prevention SaaS Platform
To fix this important security problem, we suggest creating an in-house AI-based phishing prevention SaaS platform. This system offers real-time, multi-channel defense against phishing attacks through:
Browser-based monitoring
Integrated email scanning
Enterprise API access
A centralized AI risk intelligence engine
Create a better, more detailed dashboard and tools for analysis so you can see more clearly how phishing campaigns are progressing and how secure things are.
Our service fundamentally differs from email-only solutions. By checking digital actions as they happen, we stop possible problems before they happen. The platform will be affordable, easy to grow, and work with different systems, offering strong security that's suited for small and medium businesses.
2. Literature Review
The growing sophistication of phishing attacks has driven significant research into AI-based detection systems across multiple channels. This section reviews key academic work that directly informs the design and methodology of AEGIS-ONE.
2.1 Machine Learning for Phishing URL and Website Detection
Several studies have established machine learning as the most reliable approach for phishing URL classification. Alnemari and Alshammari (2023) compared ANN, SVM, Decision Tree, and Random Forest classifiers for phishing domain detection (Alnemari & Alshammari, 2023), finding that Random Forest and ANN delivered the most consistent results across varied URL datasets. Building on this, a 2024 IEEE comparative study on phishing URL detection confirmed that Random Forest achieves 94.5% accuracy, outperforming SVM, KNN, Logistic Regression, and Naive Bayes (IEEE, 2024). For website-level detection, a 2024 IEEE Access study demonstrated that ensemble deep learning models achieve up to 99% accuracy (IEEE Access, 2024) on a dataset of over 11,000 phishing websites - directly validating the ensemble approach adopted in AEGIS-ONE's AI Risk Intelligence Engine (Module 7.2).
2.2 Deep Learning for Email Phishing Classification
Email remains the primary delivery channel for phishing attacks, and deep learning models have shown strong performance in classifying phishing email content. A 2024 comparative study published in MDPI Electronics evaluated CNN, LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and GRU architectures for phishing email detection, finding that CNN-based models achieve high classification accuracy while maintaining low false positive rates (PMC/MDPI, 2024). This supports our decision to use fine-tuned BERT/RoBERTa for email content classification combined with TF-IDF and Random Forest for header and metadata analysis in AEGIS-ONE's email module.
2.3 Browser-Based Real-Time Phishing Detection
The feasibility of browser-level phishing detection has been validated by multiple recent studies. Murtaza et al. (2025) developed a Chrome extension using RoBERTa with integrated Explainable AI (XAI), achieving 98.12% accuracy in real-time phishing email detection directly within the browser environment (Murtaza et al., 2025) - a design approach closely aligned with AEGIS-ONE's browser extension module. Further, a 2026 Nature Scientific Reports study demonstrated a Chrome extension using ML with Grey Wolf Optimizer-based feature selection for real-time deployment, specifically addressing the latency and memory constraints inherent in browser-side processing (Nature, 2026). Both studies confirm that real-time, browser-based AI phishing detection is not only technically feasible but achieves near-production accuracy.
2.4 Explainable AI (XAI) in Cybersecurity
Enterprise adoption of AI-based security tools is significantly hindered when detection decisions are opaque. Capuano et al. (2022) conducted a comprehensive survey of over 300 papers covering XAI applications across phishing, malware, intrusion detection, and fraud - establishing SHAP and LIME as the most widely validated techniques for cybersecurity explainability (IEEE Access, 2022). More recently, a 2026 MDPI study introduced the HXRF model, combining SHAP, LIME, Permutation Importance, and PDP for phishing website feature selection before Random Forest classification, achieving state-of-the-art results on a 500,000-sample dataset. These findings directly justify AEGIS-ONE's XAI module (Module 7.3), which uses SHAP and LIME to provide human-readable explanations for every detection decision.
2.5 Adversarial Robustness in Phishing Detection
A critical challenge for any ML-based security system is resilience against adversarial inputs - where attackers deliberately craft content to evade classifiers. Baniecki and Biecek (2024) surveyed adversarial attack strategies and defenses specifically targeting XAI systems, identifying ensemble methods and input preprocessing as the most effective mitigations (Baniecki & Biecek, 2024). Additionally, a 2025 MDPI study on TCN-based URL phishing detection explicitly tested adversarial robustness, finding that combining temporal convolutional networks with attention mechanisms and SHAP-based feature selection significantly improves resistance to evasion attacks. AEGIS-ONE addresses adversarial robustness through a three-layer defense: ensemble modeling (BERT + XGBoost + heuristic rules), hard rule-based blocks for known-bad patterns, and continuous retraining via Module 7.11.
2.6 Multi-Channel and AI-Generated Phishing Threats
Modern phishing has expanded well beyond email. Harihar and Potdar (2024) analyzed the full spectrum of AI-assisted phishing including LLM-generated spear-phishing emails, deepfake audio/video impersonation, and QR code-based attacks, demonstrating that single-channel defenses are fundamentally inadequate against today's threat landscape (Harihar & Potdar, 2024) . This finding is central to AEGIS-ONE's multi-channel design philosophy - covering email, browser, QR code, and behavioral anomaly detection under a single unified AI engine.
2.7 Gap Identified in Existing Literature
While the reviewed studies demonstrate strong performance in individual channels - email-only classifiers, URL-only detectors, or browser-only extensions - no existing published system integrates all four channels (email + browser + QR + behavioral) under a single AI engine with built-in XAI, pre-credential-submission interception, and an on-premise privacy-preserving deployment model. AEGIS-ONE is designed to address this specific gap.
3. Problem Statement 
Phishing attacks are still the main cause of financial and data breaches around the world, and they keep finding ways to get past usual email safety measures. Attackers use smart methods like pretending to be someone else, creating fake websites that change each time, tricking people with QR codes, and manipulating people through conversation to carry out their plans.

Most current security systems mainly check for harmful emails at the server level. There is a major problem in protection because attacks usually happen after a user deals with harmful stuff outside their email, like entering passwords on a fake website.

This problem is compounded by several factors:
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) often cannot afford complex, enterprise-grade cybersecurity systems.
Available protection tools are frequently limited to specific technological ecosystems.
There is currently no unified, real-time protection layer that spans all digital interactions.
Users receive insufficient or unclear warnings about identified threats.
Existing tools offer no transparency into why a threat was flagged - leaving users confused and admins unable to audit decisions effectively
This lack of protection is especially important when users take action, like making money decisions or typing in their login details.

To tackle this, there is a strong need for a cost-effective, easy-to-scale, and multi-channel phishing prevention solution that is designed to:
Provide detection across email, browser activity, and enterprise systems.
Monitor user interactions in real time.
Actively intercept malicious actions before a compromise occurs.
Deliver explainable AI-based risk scoring.
Be deployable as a flexible Software as a Service (SaaS) solution.
Our solution addresses this gap by creating a central AI risk intelligence system. This engine checks digital interactions right away, helping stop phishing attacks before they cause any harm.

7.8 Privacy & Data Architecture
AEGIS-ONE is designed around a local-first processing model. The browser extension monitors and analyzes web content entirely on the client device - no raw browsing data is stored or transmitted. Email scanning occurs within the organization's own network boundary. Only anonymized risk scores, domain metadata, and threat indicators are sent to the dashboard. All locally stored logs are encrypted at rest (Module 7.10). The platform follows GDPR's data minimization principle - collecting only what is strictly necessary for threat detection. Enterprise privacy policies are respected through configurable data collection controls

8. Modules 
8.1 Identity & Role Management
Secure authentication (email/password + Google OAuth) with role-based access control (User, Employee Admin,Super - Administrator). Controlled system access, secure sessions, and enterprise-level authorization management
8.2 AI Risk Intelligence Engine
An algorithm that can detect emails, URLs and Attachments as well as QR codes, login pages and behavioral clues.A core detection engine.io. Produces a risk score that incorporates real-time threat classification and confidence metrics.
8.3 Explainable AI (XAI)
Provides transparent reasoning behind every detection decision to promote trust and enterprise auditability:
- NLP Attention Mapping: Maps token attention weights directly from the MultiHeadAttentionPool layer in the DistilBERT (LoRA) + Bi-LSTM model to extract and highlight the top 3 risky keywords (e.g., "urgent", "suspended") responsible for flagging content.
- URL Category Reasoners: Decodes model classification categories (benign, defacement, phishing, malware) to present context-specific explanations directly in interactive tooltips (e.g., "AI detected credential harvesting patterns in URL parameters").
- Interactive Client Tooltips: Hovering over any risk badge (next to links or in Google Search results) renders dynamic, explainable reasoning to guide users safely.
8.4 Real-Time Monitoring 
Monitors browser activity and user behavior to identify phishing sites, malicious links, and credential harvesting attempts. Accepts threats before submitting or reconciling.All content analysis is performed locally on the client device. The extension does not store browsing history or transmit raw page content - only risk scores and flagged threat indicators are forwarded to the central dashboard.
8.5 Analytics Dashboard
Includes admin review mechanisms to validate detections and refine model performance reducing alert fatigue and improves system reliability.
8.6 Active Prevention Module
Blocks harmful links without manual intervention, quarantines objectionable content, prevents the submission of credentials, and triggers alerts. It is focused on stopping attacks before they become costly.
8.7 Incident Reporting Mechanism
Generates detailed incident reports covering attack timeline, source analysis, redirect chains, and AI reasoning - supporting compliance and investigation requirements. Includes a feedback mechanism where admins can flag false positives or negatives, which are logged and fed back into the model retraining pipeline
8.8 Enterprise Deployment Module
Enables the use of cloud SaaS, private cloud infrastructure and enterprise API integration. Targeted at enabling the implementation of scalable employee systems.
8.9 Network Security Layer
Secure server-client communication, secure API endpoints, and token-based authentication. All of the data between client and server side end to end.
8.10 Client-Side Data Protection
Implement encryption for local logs ensuring sensitive information is not openly stored on user devices.
8.11 Model Retraining & Continuous Learning
Incorporates user feedback and secure metadata sharing to improve model accuracy enabling adaptive learning without exposure of sensitive enterprise data.
8.12 Web Risk Scoring
Evaluates every visited link through the AI engine and assigns a real-time risk score displaying warnings dynamically within the user’s browser.
8.13 Adaptive Security Awareness & Simulation
Provides contextual security tips and optional attack simulations to improve awareness to avoid future risks.
8.14 False Positive & False Negative Management
Includes admin review mechanisms to validate detections and refine model performance therefore improving system reliability.
8.15 Incident Recovery & Response Support
Provides guided recovery actions in case of compromise, including containment steps and escalation alerts to administrators.

10. System Limitations/Constraints 
The system cannot guarantee 100% phishing prevention, as attackers continuously evolve techniques and zero-day attacks may bypass detection models.
Real-time monitoring depends on proper client-side installation (browser extension or agent); incomplete deployment across employee systems may reduce effectiveness.
Advanced detection methods (e.g., full screen monitoring or deep behavior tracking) may be limited by operating system or browser security restrictions.
False positives and false negatives may occur, especially in early deployment phases before sufficient feedback-based tuning.
Network latency or temporary server downtime could delay risk scoring or interception in real-time scenarios.
Enterprise privacy policies may restrict certain data collection, limiting behavioral analysis depth.
Integration complexity may vary depending on existing enterprise infrastructure (custom email servers, legacy systems, etc.). 
Deepfake voice phishing (vishing), AI-generated document fraud detection, and OTP-based social engineering are outside the current system scope and are identified as planned future extensions.
11. Data Gathering Approach 
Due to the complexity of phishing threats and enterprise security requirements, a structured data gathering strategy is essential for model training and system improvement.
Our data collection approach includes:
Named public datasets: PhishTank, OpenPhish , UCI ML Phishing Dataset ,CEAS 2008 + SpamAssassin Corpus. Class imbalance handled via SMOTE augmentation.
URL and domain metadata (WHOIS data, registration age, hosting information).
Email content pattern datasets (for spam and phishing classification training).
User feedback signals (false positive/false negative reports from enterprise users).
Behavioral metadata (non-sensitive interaction patterns for anomaly detection).
Internal system analytics (threat trends, risk score distribution, blocked attempts).
All named datasets are publicly available. Pre-processing pipelines will be developed to clean, normalize, and merge these sources into a unified training corpus with balanced class distribution.All collected data will follow strict privacy controls and encryption standards to ensure compliance and enterprise trust.Additionally, technical guidance and architectural validation will be conducted under academic supervision to ensure system feasibility, scalability, and security robustness.
